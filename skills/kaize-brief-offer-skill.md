## THE THREE BUCKETS OF SPONSORED POSTS

Every sponsored brief falls into one of these three post shapes. Pick before writing.

### 1/ The Standalone Announcement Post
- Full post about the product from scratch
- Used for launches, funding rounds, major releases
- 8-15 lines typically
- Examples: Polsia, Fluidstack, Octen, Ramp Router, Adaline

### 2/ The Quote Tweet (QRT)
- Reacts to their official announcement post
- Shorter, more personal
- Add own caption above their post
- Examples: Antioch, Adaline, Tilt Rips, HeyGen, HyperFrames

### 3/ The Native/Ad-Wrapped Post
- Mentions the product organically inside a broader post
- Often trojan-horsed inside a "value" post
- Example: Fable 5 clipping (Vadim article), Creao x MiniMax, Kimi K3 hack + Creao

## THE CANONICAL POST STRUCTURE

Every good sponsored post follows this skeleton. Diverge only when the brief demands it.

```
1/ HOOK — one shocking line (number, contrast, or POV)
2/ CONTEXT — 1-2 lines connecting hook to the product
3/ PRODUCT INTRODUCTION — "@product just did X"
4/ WHAT IT DOES — 3-5 bullet points
5/ WHY IT MATTERS — 1-2 lines of insight
6/ CTA — link + one bait line
```

### Real example (Ramp Router):

```
Everyone wires ONE FRONTIER model into their app and calls it a day
                                                              ← HOOK
And it works fine until you're spending THOUSANDS OF DOLLARS 
every month - even though you could have done it for $2
                                                              ← CONTEXT

However, there is a solution - @tryramp Router
                                                              ← PRODUCT

A closed beta gateway between your app and the model market:
- One common path to models
- Visibility into what each request actually costs
- Tests of model fit on real workloads
- OpenAI-compatible, one key, one endpoint
                                                              ← WHAT IT DOES

The point isn't to use AI less. The point is to use it wisely.
                                                              ← WHY IT MATTERS

Only a small number of builders will be approved:
[link]
                                                              ← CTA
```

## THE 5 CANONICAL ANGLES (from Antioch brief, universally applicable)

Every brief will offer 3-6 angles. These 5 archetypes cover 90% of them:

### 1/ Been-there pain
Address a pain the audience feels every day. Product = solution.
```
Everyone wires ONE FRONTIER model into their app and calls it a day
And it works fine until you're spending THOUSANDS OF DOLLARS
```

### 2/ Reformed skeptic
"I used to think X. Turns out Y." — signals honesty + expertise.
```
I've been skeptical of "smart routing" claims. Choosing the 
cheapest model is easy. Proving it hit the quality bar is hard
```

### 3/ Insider observation
Position as someone who sees a hidden truth about the market.
```
The question is no longer "how do I access more models" 
It is "what level of intelligence does this work need"
```

### 4/ Talent signal
Frame around who's building it, who's backing it, why it matters for careers.
```
There's a version of the next four years where this is the 
most consequential place to work
```

### 5/ Contrarian / hot take
Take an unexpected position on a widely accepted opinion.
```
Personalization is cope. Your cold DM didn't fail because 
you forgot to compliment their podcast
```

## LIST FORMATTING RULES

### Bullet formats to use:
```
- Bullet with dash
- Another bullet
```

```
> arrow bullet (kaize signature)
> another arrow bullet
```

```
1/ Numbered slash
2/ Another one
```

### Never:
- Use emoji bullets (•, ✅, 🚀) in sponsored posts unless brief demands
- Use bullet points inside a QRT reply (X algo penalizes it)
- Mix bullet types in one post

### The "structured data" format for specs:
```
[ WHAT I BUILT ]: X
[ HOW ]: Y
[ TIME TO BUILD ]: Z
[ COST ]: W
```
Best for personal use cases and reviews.

## DISCLOSURE COMPLIANCE (MANDATORY)

Every sponsored post on X requires:

```
1/ Toggle Paid Partnership disclosure (flag icon)
   → NOT a text-only "#ad" — text-only fails
2/ Toggle Made with AI if AI was used in writing
3/ Disclosure must be visible IMMEDIATELY
   → NOT buried in hashtags, comments, or after "see more"
```

Other platforms:
- Start with "Ad:", "Paid partnership with [Brand]", or "Sponsored by [Brand]"

Video:
- Say disclosure OUT LOUD + text overlay

Financial campaigns:
- Include "This is not financial advice. Always do your own research."

## THINGS TO NEVER DO

- Never repeat their announcement copy word-for-word
- Never claim guaranteed savings/returns without approval
- Never attack competitors by name unless brief allows
- Never bury the disclosure
- Never post before AM approves the draft
- Never break "closed beta" framing if that's what brief says
- Never claim features from "early access" as generally available
- Never invent stats — only use brief-provided proof points

## THE "PROOF POINT" RULE

Every claim must be backed by a proof point from the brief. Use MAX 1-2 per post.

### Good:
```
AI token spend across Ramp's customers grew 20.7x since 
January 2025
```
(one clear stat, from their approved list)

### Bad:
```
Ramp has 70,000+ customers, $100B+ in purchases, saved 
$12B for clients, tokens grew 20.7x, and processes 27M 
hours saved...
```
(too many stats, dilutes the impact)

## CAMPAIGN-SPECIFIC PATTERNS I'VE USED

### For AI infrastructure / tools:
- Lead with the developer pain point
- One benchmark number in the hook
- Focus on cost or speed advantage
- Never claim universal solution

### For AI video / creative:
- Lead with the founder use case
- "Vibe-coding" analogy works
- GIF required (per most briefs)
- Frame as "AI creative team" not "AI clip generator"

### For B2B / GTM tools:
- Lead with a painful sales/marketing scenario
- Use contrast ("before X" vs "after X")
- Include one shocking behavioral stat
- Aspirational close for founders

### For funding announcements:
- Numbers in first line (raise + valuation)
- Investor list as flex
- Bigger thesis about the market
- End with talent signal

### For AI model launches:
- Benchmarks in the first 3 lines
- Comparison to established models
- Include the honest limitations paragraph
- Free credit / trial CTA

### For crypto / trading:
- Prize pool in the hook
- Numbered guide (how to enter)
- Clear timeline
- Add compliance disclaimer if needed

## COMMON BRIEF STRUCTURE (WHAT TO EXPECT)

Almost every brief follows this shape:

```
1/ Disclosure guidelines (always same)
2/ Your job (always same)
3/ Post details / timing
4/ Context / what it is
5/ Content angles (pick 1)
6/ Example captions (never copy, use as reference)
7/ Do's and Don'ts / guardrails
8/ Links & tags
```

Read in this order:
1. Timing (when to post)
2. Required disclosures
3. Angles (pick one)
4. Guardrails (what you can't claim)
5. Example captions (get vibe, don't copy)
6. Then write

## THE DRAFT REVIEW LOOP

Standard flow:

```
1/ Write draft using this skill
2/ Send to Account Manager (AM) for review
3/ AM sends approval or edits
4/ Post at approved time
5/ Toggle disclosures
6/ Include tags + tracked link
7/ QRT approved launch post if required
```

Don't post before AM approves. Don't skip the disclosure toggles.

## THE "PERSONAL INTERPRETATION" MODE

When feedback says "too descriptive, more personal" (Polsia feedback pattern):

### Do:
- Start with POV — "I work in..."
- Add "honestly," "actually," "here's what I think"
- Include personal experience — "founders I talk to"
- Take a position that could be argued with

### Don't:
- Retell the story linearly
- Just summarize their news
- Stay neutral

### The pivot from summary to POV:
```
BAD (summary):
"Polsia's bill grew from $500K to $1.2M as users increased. 
Ben spent 2 months rebuilding the cost stack..."

GOOD (POV):
"I work in AI marketing. Every founder I talk to obsesses 
over growth. Nobody talks about the invoice underneath it. 
Polsia's story is why."
```

## VIRALITY CHECKLIST (from Polsia brief, universal)

Before posting, check:
- [ ] Numbers in the first line (not third paragraph)
- [ ] One clear tension (not three)
- [ ] Takes a position (not neutral summary)
- [ ] Quotable ending (not "click here to learn more")
- [ ] Under 15 lines unless brief allows more
- [ ] Personal POV visible in first 3 lines
- [ ] Product mentioned by tag (not just name)
- [ ] Link at bottom, not middle

## RUSSIAN TRANSLATIONS

When translating posts to Russian:

### Rules:
- Keep tags in original (@handle)
- Keep company names in Latin (Anthropic, not Антропик)
- Keep dollar signs and technical terms in English
- Punctuation stays Russian style
- Same lowercase-first rhythm

### Example transition:
```
EN: "@polsia_ai just got hit with a $1.2M/mo bill"
RU: "@polsia_ai получили счёт на $1.2M/мес"
```

## WHEN THINGS GO WRONG

### If AM says "too much like an ad":
- Add stronger POV opener
- Include a limitation or honest caveat
- Add "reformed skeptic" angle

### If AM says "too descriptive":
- Remove chronological retelling
- Add personal reaction / opinion
- Cut the summary paragraphs

### If AM says "too long":
- Cut context down to 1-2 lines
- Reduce bullets to 3-4
- Combine sentences

### If AM says "not clear enough":
- Add "here's what happened:" transition
- Make the "so what" explicit
- Add specific numbers earlier
